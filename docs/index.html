1 · Introduction

Diwali, stubble burning and Delhi's air

Our question: how much of the October–November rise in PM2.5 in Delhi comes from Diwali night, and how much from the season's weather?

  • Every autumn Delhi's air turns grey. Two explanations dominate the news: Diwali fireworks and the season, meaning colder, stiller air plus smoke from crop-residue burning in Punjab and Haryana.
  • PM2.5 = particles under 2.5 micrometres, small enough to reach deep into the lungs, measured in µg/m³. India's 24-hour standard is 60; the WHO guideline is 15.
  • We study three Diwalis, 12 Nov 2023, 31 Oct 2024 and 20 Oct 2025, with hourly data from 11 government stations, 15 September to 30 November each year.

2 · Data and methods

Three public sources joined into one hourly table: 11 stations × 3 seasons × 1,848 hours = 60,984 station-hours, 20 columns.

CPCB CAAQMS Data Repository

Hourly PM2.5 at 12 Delhi stations, 2023–2025. The main source for all three Diwalis.

36 yearly CSV files, downloaded by hand (the site has a captcha). airquality.cpcb.gov.in

OpenAQ

Station list and 15-minute PM2.5 for 2025. Used to cross-check CPCB and fill its gaps; it holds no Delhi government data for 2023–24.

1,008 daily files via API v3 and the AWS archive (script). docs.openaq.org

Open-Meteo Historical Weather

Hourly temperature, humidity, rain, wind and boundary-layer height for central Delhi.

3 API calls, one per season (script). open-meteo.com

A look at the clean table

Six rows from Diwali night 2025. Every row is one station in one hour; the weather columns are the same for every station in that hour.

How we cleaned it

  • Formats: units moved out of column names, text dates parsed (0 failures), both PM2.5 sources put on Indian time, OpenAQ's 15-minute readings averaged to hours, station wind converted from m/s to km/h.
  • Duplicates: CPCB and OpenAQ name stations differently ("Pusa, Delhi - IMD" vs "Pusa, Delhi - IITM"), so we matched stations by their values: 11 of 12 agree hour for hour in ≥80% of hours. We dropped 20,120 duplicate OpenAQ hours and merged the two Pusa monitors (same location) into one site.
  • Outliers: removed 55 impossible values (≤ 0), 43 stuck-sensor hours and 28 single-station spikes; kept 154 extreme hours seen across the city, such as Diwali nights.
  • Missing values: about 3% of hours, linked to station and season, not to pollution level (MAR). Gaps ≤ 3 hours interpolated (482 hours); longer gaps left empty (1,487 hours, 2.4%).

How we answer the question

  1. Seasonal rise: city-wide average in October–November minus 15–30 September (city values need 8 of 11 stations).
  2. Weather's part: two regressions of daily log PM2.5: ventilation only (mixing height, wind) as a lower estimate, and full weather (adding temperature, humidity) as an upper estimate.
  3. Diwali night's part: Diwali night (18:00–06:00) minus the nights 2–7 days either side, and minus what its weather predicts. The 12-hour excess is spread over the 1,464 hours of October–November.

robots.txt and terms checked for every site; full code, cleaning log and data dictionary are in the repository.

4 · Conclusion

Diwali night accounts for about 1–4% of Delhi's October–November PM2.5 rise. The other 96–99% comes with the season.

+126 to +133 µg/m³seasonal rise (Oct–Nov minus late Sep) in 2023, 2024, 2025
+1.4 to +5.3 µg/m³added to the Oct–Nov average by Diwali night: 1.1–4.2% of the rise
3–41% / 72–122%of the rise predicted by ventilation alone / by the full weather model
1.8× to 8.4×Diwali night against what its weather predicted (463, 382, 785 µg/m³)
  • Diwali night is the worst event of the autumn: city-wide PM2.5 peaked at 679, 650 and 1,331 µg/m³, about 22 times India's limit in 2025.
  • But it is short. 12 hours out of the 1,464 in October–November, so it moves the season's average by only a few µg/m³.
  • The season does the rest. In November 98% of days were "very poor" or "severe". Weather explains somewhere between a small part (3–41%) and nearly all (72–122%) of the rise; our data cannot separate colder, stiller air from the stubble smoke and heating that arrive at the same time of year.

5 · Limitations

  • No stubble-burning data, so "the season" mixes weather, crop-fire smoke, heating and traffic. That is why the weather share is a range.
  • Modelled weather at one point for the whole city (Open-Meteo). Station thermometers agree with it (r = 0.87), but station wind sensors were too unreliable to replace it.
  • Only three Diwalis, and 2025 behaved very differently from 2023 and 2024.
  • Possible capping before 2025: no 2023–24 reading exceeds about 1,000 µg/m³, while nine stations did at once in 2025, so older Diwali peaks may be underestimated.
  • Station choice: 12 of CPCB's 59 Delhi stations (11 sites), chosen to cover the city.
  • Association, not cause: the regressions show which weather goes with dirtier air, not what causes it.
